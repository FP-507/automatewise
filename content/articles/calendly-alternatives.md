## Calendly's Free Plan Is One Event Type

That single limit drives most searches for an alternative. One event type means you cannot offer a fifteen-minute intro and a sixty-minute consultation without paying $12 per user monthly. Add a team and the cost multiplies for a feature set that has not changed much in years.

The other common reasons are branding, since Calendly's logo appears on free bookings, and data residency for teams that cannot route client calendars through a US processor.

## 1. Cal.com — The Open Source Replacement

**Choose if:** You want unlimited event types free, or you need to self-host.

Cal.com is Calendly rebuilt as open source. The hosted free tier allows unlimited event types, which alone solves the most common complaint. Self-hosting removes per-user cost entirely and keeps booking data on your infrastructure, which matters for regulated industries.

It supports workflows, routing forms, team scheduling, and payment collection, matching most of Calendly's paid features.

**What you give up:** some polish, and self-hosting means maintenance. The hosted version at $15 per user monthly is not cheaper than Calendly, so the savings come from the free tier or self-hosting.

## 2. Google Calendar Appointment Schedules — Free and Already There

**Choose if:** You use Google Workspace and need straightforward booking.

Appointment schedules are included with Google Workspace and personal Google accounts. Set availability, share a link, bookings land directly in your calendar with Meet links attached. No extra subscription.

**What you give up:** routing logic, custom branding, payment collection, and team round-robin. This is basic booking, done competently. See our [Google Workspace automation guide](/blog/automate-google-workspace).

## 3. Microsoft Bookings — Included With Microsoft 365

**Choose if:** Your organization runs Microsoft 365.

Bookings is included with most Microsoft 365 business plans, supports staff scheduling and service catalogues, and integrates with Outlook and Teams natively.

**What you give up:** the polish and third-party integrations Calendly offers. Setup is also less intuitive.

## 4. TidyCal — Pay Once

**Choose if:** You object to subscriptions more than to features.

TidyCal is frequently available as a lifetime deal around $29. It covers multiple booking types, calendar connections, and payments. For solo operators, paying once replaces $144 annually.

**What you give up:** advanced routing and team features. It is a solid basic tool, not a platform.

## 5. SavvyCal — Better for the Person Booking

**Choose if:** You send booking links to clients and find Calendly's experience impersonal.

SavvyCal lets recipients overlay their own calendar on your availability, which makes finding a time genuinely faster. Around $12 monthly, matching Calendly, but the booking experience is meaningfully better.

**What you give up:** nothing in price, so this is a quality choice rather than a cost one.

## 6. Zoho Bookings — Cheapest Paid Option

**Choose if:** You already use Zoho, or price is the deciding factor.

Around $6 per user monthly, roughly half Calendly, with CRM and Desk integration if you run the Zoho suite. Free for a single user.

**What you give up:** ecosystem breadth outside Zoho.

## 7. Acuity Scheduling — For Appointment Businesses

**Choose if:** You run a salon, clinic, studio, or anything with recurring client appointments.

Acuity handles intake forms, packages, memberships, deposits, and class scheduling that generic tools do not. Owned by Squarespace, from $20 monthly.

**What you give up:** simplicity and price for pure meeting scheduling. See our [appointment scheduling automation guide](/blog/automate-appointment-scheduling).

## When Calendly Still Wins

- **Integrations matter.** Calendly connects to more CRMs and automation platforms natively than any alternative, as covered in our [Calendly automation tutorial](/blog/calendly-automation-tutorial).
- **Recipients recognize it.** Familiarity reduces friction when booking with people outside your company.
- **You need team routing.** Round-robin, collective availability, and routing forms are mature here and half-built elsewhere.
- **One event type is enough.** Many solo users never hit the free limit.

## What is the best free alternative to Calendly?

Cal.com's free tier allows unlimited event types, which directly solves Calendly free's single biggest limitation, and it can be self-hosted at no cost beyond a server. If you use Google Workspace, appointment schedules are already included and require no new tool. Microsoft 365 users have Bookings included similarly. Cal.com is the best choice when you need real scheduling features free; Google and Microsoft options are best when basic booking suffices.

## Is Cal.com really open source?

Yes. Cal.com's code is publicly available and self-hostable under the AGPLv3 license, with a commercial license available for organizations that need different terms. Self-hosting gives unlimited users and event types for the cost of a server, roughly $10 to $20 monthly, and keeps booking data on your own infrastructure. The hosted version at $15 per user monthly is a convenience product; the savings come from self-hosting or from the generous free tier.

## Can I use Google Calendar instead of Calendly?

For basic booking, yes. Google Calendar's appointment schedules let you publish availability, share a booking link, and have meetings created automatically with Google Meet links, at no additional cost with a Google account or Workspace plan. What it lacks is conditional routing, custom branding, payment collection, team round-robin, and reminder workflows. If your scheduling is one person offering a small number of meeting types, it fully replaces Calendly's free tier.
