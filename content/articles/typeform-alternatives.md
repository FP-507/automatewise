## Ten Responses a Month Is Not a Free Plan

Typeform's free tier allows ten responses monthly. The Basic plan at $29 raises that to one hundred. For a form collecting leads from a website, one hundred responses is a light week, and the next tier jumps to $59.

That pricing is the reason almost everyone looks elsewhere. The forms themselves are excellent, which is why this guide is honest about what you lose.

## Quick Comparison

| Platform | Free tier | Entry price | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tally | Unlimited responses | $29/mo | Almost everything, free |
| Google Forms | Unlimited | Free | Internal and simple use |
| Fillout | 1,000 responses/mo | $15/mo | Logic and integrations |
| Formbricks | Unlimited (self-hosted) | Free | Open source, privacy |
| Jotform | 100 responses/mo | $34/mo | Payments, complex forms |
| Framer or Webflow forms | Included | Included | Already using the builder |
| Formspree | 50 submissions/mo | $10/mo | Custom-coded forms |

## 1. Tally — The Obvious Answer

**Choose if:** You want Typeform's experience without the response cap.

Tally allows unlimited responses on its free plan. Not a thousand, unlimited. It supports conditional logic, calculations, payments, file uploads, and integrations, most of which Typeform charges for. The editor works like Notion, typing "/" to insert blocks, which makes building fast.

Paid plans around $29 monthly add custom domains, branding removal, and team features.

**What you give up:** Typeform's animation polish and its larger native integration catalogue. Functionally, very little else.

## 2. Google Forms — Free and Universal

**Choose if:** The form is internal, or aesthetics do not matter.

Unlimited responses, unlimited forms, no cost, and responses flow straight into Google Sheets. Paired with [Apps Script](/blog/google-apps-script-tutorial) it handles notification and routing logic without any third-party tool.

**What you give up:** design quality and conditional logic depth. Google Forms looks like Google Forms, which is fine internally and weak for public-facing lead capture. See our [Google Forms automation guide](/blog/google-forms-automation).

## 3. Fillout — Strongest Logic in the Category

**Choose if:** Your forms need real branching and native database writes.

Fillout offers 1,000 responses monthly free and handles multi-page forms, complex conditional logic, and direct integration with Airtable, Notion, and databases, writing records without an intermediate automation step.

**What you give up:** brand recognition, and a smaller community when you need help.

## 4. Formbricks — Open Source and Self-Hosted

**Choose if:** Response data cannot sit on a third-party server.

Formbricks is open source, self-hostable, and unlimited. For healthcare, legal, or EU organizations with strict data requirements, this removes the compliance question entirely.

**What you give up:** hosting effort, and a less refined editor.

## 5. Jotform — When Forms Get Complicated

**Choose if:** You need payments, signatures, HIPAA compliance, or approval workflows.

Jotform handles complex form requirements that simpler tools do not: conditional pricing, multi-party signatures, prefill, and regulated data handling. Free tier allows 100 responses monthly.

**What you give up:** simplicity and price at $34 monthly for the entry paid plan.

## 6. Your Website Builder's Forms

**Choose if:** You already pay for Webflow, Framer, or Squarespace.

These include form handling with submissions stored and emailed at no extra cost. For a contact form, adding a separate tool is unnecessary spending.

**What you give up:** logic, calculations, and sophisticated routing.

## 7. Formspree — For Developers

**Choose if:** You write your own HTML and only need somewhere for submissions to go.

Point a form's action at Formspree and it handles storage, spam filtering, and notifications. From $10 monthly, with 50 submissions free.

**What you give up:** any form builder at all. You write the markup.

## When Typeform Earns Its Price

- **Conversion matters more than cost.** One question at a time genuinely lifts completion rates on long forms. If a form drives revenue, the difference can exceed the subscription.
- **Brand perception counts.** For customer research, application forms, or anything client-facing at a premium price point, Typeform still looks the most considered.
- **You use the integrations.** Its native catalogue is broader than the alternatives here, though [Make](/blog/getting-started-with-make) and [Zapier](/blog/getting-started-with-zapier) close that gap for any of them. See our [Typeform automation tutorial](/blog/typeform-automation-tutorial).

For most teams, Tally delivers ninety percent of Typeform at zero cost, and that trade is easy.

## What is the best free Typeform alternative?

Tally, without much competition. Its free plan allows unlimited responses, plus conditional logic, calculations, file uploads, and payments, most of which Typeform restricts to paid tiers. Google Forms is also unlimited and free but noticeably plainer, which suits internal use more than public lead capture. Fillout offers 1,000 responses monthly free with the strongest conditional logic of the three. Start with Tally unless you need Fillout's database integrations.

## Why is Typeform so expensive?

Typeform charges by response volume, and its free tier caps at ten responses monthly with Basic at $29 allowing one hundred. A form receiving modest website traffic exhausts that quickly, pushing users to $59 or higher. The pricing reflects Typeform's design quality and conversational format, which genuinely improve completion rates on longer forms. Whether that is worth it depends on whether your forms drive revenue; for internal surveys and simple contact forms it rarely is.

## Can I connect Tally or Google Forms to my CRM?

Yes. Both support webhooks and native integrations, and both connect through [Make](/blog/getting-started-with-make), [Zapier](/blog/getting-started-with-zapier), or [n8n](/blog/getting-started-with-n8n) to any CRM. The usual pattern is: form submission triggers the automation, it searches the CRM for an existing contact by email, then creates or updates the record and notifies your team. Setup takes about fifteen minutes. See our [lead generation automation guide](/blog/automate-lead-generation) for the full workflow.
