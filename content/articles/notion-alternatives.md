## Notion's Problems Are Speed, Offline, and Ownership

Notion does a great deal well, which is why the complaints are specific rather than general. Pages load slowly once a workspace grows. Offline access is unreliable. Everything lives on Notion's servers in a proprietary format. And for teams that only wanted a wiki, the database machinery is overhead they never use.

Which of those bothers you determines where to go, because the alternatives split cleanly into local-first tools, faster wikis, and stronger databases.

## 1. Obsidian — Your Files, Your Machine

**Choose if:** Speed, offline access, or data ownership is the issue.

Obsidian stores everything as plain Markdown files in a folder on your computer. Pages open instantly regardless of vault size, everything works offline, and your notes remain readable in any text editor twenty years from now. Free for personal use; official sync costs $50 annually, though you can use any cloud folder instead.

**What you give up:** real-time collaboration and Notion's databases. Obsidian is a personal knowledge tool that teams bolt collaboration onto, not the reverse.

## 2. Coda — Documents That Act Like Software

**Choose if:** You liked Notion's databases and wanted them to do more.

Coda's tables support real formulas across documents, buttons that trigger actions, and packs that connect to external services. Where Notion databases store and display, Coda documents compute and act. Pricing is per document maker rather than per user, so read-only viewers are free, which often costs less than Notion for larger teams.

**What you give up:** simplicity. Coda's power surfaces as complexity quickly.

## 3. Confluence — When It Is Really a Company Wiki

**Choose if:** Your Notion workspace is documentation for more than fifty people.

Confluence handles permissions, page hierarchies, approval workflows, and search at organizational scale better than Notion does, and integrates natively with [Jira](/blog/jira-automation-tutorial). Free for up to ten users, then around $5.16 per user monthly.

**What you give up:** flexibility and visual appeal. Confluence is enterprise documentation software and feels like it.

## 4. Anytype — Local-First With Collaboration

**Choose if:** You want Notion's structure without Notion's servers.

Anytype is local-first and end-to-end encrypted, with objects, relations, and multiple views that resemble Notion's model closely. Data lives on your device and syncs peer to peer. It is free.

**What you give up:** maturity. The ecosystem and integrations are still thin compared with Notion's.

## 5. Craft — For People Who Actually Write

**Choose if:** Your Notion pages are documents, not databases, and they look bad.

Craft produces genuinely beautiful documents with excellent export and strong Apple platform integration. Around $10 monthly.

**What you give up:** databases, which Craft handles only lightly. This is a writing tool.

## 6. Airtable — When Databases Were the Point

**Choose if:** You used Notion databases and found them underpowered.

Stronger field types, real formula depth, better views, and a proper automation engine with scripting. See our [Airtable automation guide](/blog/airtable-automation-guide) and [Notion vs Airtable](/blog/notion-vs-airtable).

**What you give up:** documents. Airtable stores records, not pages, and costs $20 per user monthly.

## 7. ClickUp — Project Management With Docs Attached

**Choose if:** Notion is where your team writes but the actual work tracking happens elsewhere.

ClickUp Docs sit alongside tasks, sprints, goals, and dashboards, consolidating two tools at around $7 per user monthly. See our [ClickUp automation tutorial](/blog/clickup-automation-tutorial).

**What you give up:** document quality. ClickUp Docs are functional rather than pleasant.

## When Notion Is Still Right

- **You need one tool for several jobs.** Wiki, database, and project tracker in a single workspace remains Notion's real advantage, and splitting that across three tools usually costs more.
- **Your team is already fluent.** Migration burns weeks and breaks every internal link.
- **Free tier covers you.** For individuals and small teams, Notion's free plan is generous.
- **Automation is connected.** Notion's API works well with [Make](/blog/getting-started-with-make) and [n8n](/blog/getting-started-with-n8n), as covered in our [Notion automation guide](/blog/notion-automation-guide).

If speed is the only complaint, try archiving old pages and reducing database view complexity before migrating. Large workspaces often recover most of their responsiveness.

## What is the best free Notion alternative?

Obsidian is free for personal use and stores notes as local Markdown files, which makes it faster than Notion and fully offline. Anytype is free, local-first, and end-to-end encrypted with a structure closer to Notion's objects and relations. For teams, Confluence is free up to ten users and ClickUp's free tier includes docs alongside project management. Choose Obsidian for personal knowledge work and Anytype if you want Notion's database model without the cloud dependency.

## Is Obsidian better than Notion?

For personal knowledge management, speed, and privacy, yes. Obsidian opens pages instantly, works fully offline, and stores plain Markdown you own permanently. For team collaboration, Notion is clearly better: Obsidian has no real-time multi-user editing, no comments, and no permissions model. The honest split is that Obsidian is a personal tool and Notion is a team tool. Many people use both, keeping personal notes in Obsidian and shared documentation in Notion.

## Can I export my data out of Notion?

Yes, though imperfectly. Notion exports to Markdown, CSV, HTML, and PDF from workspace settings. Databases become CSV files and pages become Markdown. What breaks is internal linking, nested page structure, and database relations, which typically need manual repair after import. Obsidian and Anytype both offer Notion importers that handle much of this automatically. Export before you need it so you know what your data actually looks like outside Notion.
