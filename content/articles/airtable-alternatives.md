## Per-User Pricing Is What Breaks Airtable

Airtable's free plan caps at 1,000 records per base, which is fine until it is not. The Team plan at $20 per user monthly then multiplies across everyone who needs read access, including people who only ever look at a dashboard. A ten-person team pays $2,400 a year for what often amounts to a shared spreadsheet with better field types.

The second reason people leave is the opposite problem: they need a real database, with proper relations and query performance, and Airtable is not one.

## 1. NocoDB — Airtable Without the Meter

**Choose if:** You like Airtable specifically and object only to the price.

NocoDB puts an Airtable-style interface on top of a real database, Postgres or MySQL, and is free to self-host with unlimited records and users. It supports grid, gallery, kanban, and form views, plus relations and rollups. For teams whose objection is purely cost, this is the closest substitute available.

**What you give up:** Airtable's polish, its automation engine, and its extension ecosystem. You also run the server.

## 2. Baserow — Open Source with a Managed Option

**Choose if:** You want open source but would rather pay someone to host it.

Baserow is open source, self-hostable for free, and offers managed cloud from around $5 per user monthly, a quarter of Airtable's Team price. Its API is clean and it connects readily to automation platforms.

**What you give up:** a smaller feature set and fewer view types than Airtable.

## 3. Notion — When Data Belongs Next to Documents

**Choose if:** Your Airtable bases are mostly lists that need context around them.

Notion databases support relations, rollups, and multiple views, and every record is also a page that can hold full documentation. For content calendars, CRMs, and project trackers that need notes attached, this consolidates two tools into one at $10 per user monthly.

**What you give up:** Airtable's stronger field types, formula depth, and automation. See [Notion vs Airtable](/blog/notion-vs-airtable) and our [Notion database automation guide](/blog/notion-database-automation).

## 4. Google Sheets — The Honest Downgrade

**Choose if:** Your base is under a few thousand rows and one or two people maintain it.

Free with any Google account, universally understood, and connects to every automation platform. Paired with [Apps Script](/blog/google-apps-script-tutorial) it handles more than most people expect.

**What you give up:** field type enforcement, relations, concurrent editing safety, and performance past roughly 10,000 rows. Our [spreadsheet vs database guide](/blog/spreadsheet-vs-database-for-business) covers when this is a mistake.

## 5. SeaTable — Familiar and Cheaper

**Choose if:** You want an Airtable-like product at about a third of the price.

SeaTable offers a comparable interface with 25,000 rows free and paid plans from around $7 per user monthly. European hosting is available, which matters for GDPR-sensitive teams.

**What you give up:** ecosystem size and community resources.

## 6. Supabase — When You Actually Need a Database

**Choose if:** Airtable was always a workaround and your data deserves Postgres.

Supabase is hosted Postgres with auto-generated APIs, authentication, and storage, at $25 monthly flat rather than per user. Proper SQL, real indexes, and no record caps. If you are building a product rather than tracking work, this is the correct destination.

**What you give up:** the no-code interface. Supabase expects developers, though its table editor is usable for simple edits.

## 7. Monday.com — Project Work, Not Data Storage

**Choose if:** Your Airtable bases are really project trackers.

Monday's board model, automation recipes, and dashboards suit work management better than generic databases. Pricing starts around $9 per user monthly.

**What you give up:** flexibility as a general datastore. See our [Monday automation tutorial](/blog/monday-automation-tutorial).

## When Airtable Is Worth the Money

- **Automation matters.** Airtable's built-in automation with scripting is genuinely strong, and rebuilding it elsewhere costs more than the subscription. See our [Airtable automation guide](/blog/airtable-automation-guide).
- **Your team is non-technical.** Nothing in the open-source column matches Airtable's approachability.
- **Interfaces earn their keep.** Interface Designer produces usable internal apps without code.
- **The team is small.** Under five people, $20 each is cheaper than the hours spent migrating and self-hosting.

## What is the best free Airtable alternative?

NocoDB and Baserow are both free to self-host with unlimited records and users, and NocoDB deliberately mirrors Airtable's interface, which makes migration straightforward. Both need a server, roughly $10 monthly. Without self-hosting, SeaTable offers 25,000 rows free and Notion's free plan is generous for personal use. Google Sheets remains free and connects to every [automation platform](/blog/best-automation-tools-small-business), though it lacks relations and field-type enforcement.

## Why is Airtable so expensive?

Airtable charges per user, and that includes people who only view data. A ten-person team on the Team plan pays $200 monthly, or $2,400 annually, even if seven of them never edit anything. Alternatives avoid this in two ways: self-hosted tools like NocoDB and Baserow have no per-user cost, and platforms like Supabase charge a flat rate regardless of team size. Audit how many of your seats actually edit records before assuming you need Airtable's pricing model.

## Can Notion replace Airtable?

For most small-team use, yes. Notion databases support relations, rollups, filters, and multiple views, and cost $10 per user monthly against Airtable's $20. The advantage is that records are also pages, so documentation lives with the data. Airtable remains stronger for complex formulas, strict field types, larger datasets, and its built-in automation with scripting. Choose Notion when context and documentation matter more than data rigor. See [Notion vs Airtable](/blog/notion-vs-airtable).
