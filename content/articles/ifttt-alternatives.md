## IFTTT Got Smaller While Everything Else Got Bigger

IFTTT once ran unlimited applets for free. Now the free tier allows two, Pro costs $3.49 monthly, and the applet library has thinned as partners dropped support. Meanwhile competitors added conditional logic, multi-step flows, and local execution that IFTTT never built.

Most people looking for an alternative fall into one of two camps: smart home users who hit the applet limit, or business users who outgrew simple trigger-action pairs. The right answer is completely different for each.

## For Smart Home Users

### 1. Home Assistant — The Serious Upgrade

**Choose if:** Smart home is your main use and you want devices to work without the internet.

Home Assistant runs on your own hardware, a Raspberry Pi is enough, and controls devices locally. That means automations continue during an internet outage and your device data never reaches a third party. It supports thousands of integrations, far more device types than IFTTT, and its automation engine handles conditions, loops, and time windows that IFTTT cannot express.

**What you give up:** a weekend of setup and ongoing tinkering. This is a hobby as much as a tool. Our [IFTTT smart home guide](/blog/ifttt-smart-home-automation) covers the simpler path if that appeals more.

### 2. Apple Shortcuts — Free and Already Installed

**Choose if:** You are in Apple's ecosystem and your automations are personal.

Shortcuts handles device triggers, location, time, NFC tags, and app actions with no subscription. Personal automations run on your iPhone without any cloud service. For "when I arrive home, turn on lights and start a playlist," it replaces IFTTT entirely and costs nothing.

**What you give up:** anything involving non-Apple devices or web services beyond what Shortcuts supports natively.

### 3. Google Home Routines — The Zero-Effort Option

**Choose if:** Your devices are Google-compatible and your needs are basic.

Routines cover voice-triggered and scheduled actions across Google-compatible devices at no cost. Setup takes minutes.

**What you give up:** conditional logic, third-party web services, and anything beyond straightforward device control. See our [IFTTT with Google Assistant and Alexa guide](/blog/ifttt-google-assistant-alexa) for how the two compare.

## For Business Users

### 4. Make — The Natural Step Up

**Choose if:** You were using IFTTT for business and kept hitting its ceiling.

Make gives you branching, loops, error handling, and data transformation that IFTTT simply does not have, at $10.59 monthly for 10,000 operations. For anyone running applets that move customer data, log form submissions, or sync tools, this is the correct destination.

**What you give up:** IFTTT's simplicity. Make asks you to think in flowcharts. See the [Make tutorial](/blog/make-tutorial-step-by-step) and [IFTTT vs Zapier vs Make](/blog/ifttt-vs-zapier-vs-make).

### 5. Zapier — When You Need Every App

**Choose if:** Your business tools are niche and you want the widest integration library.

Roughly 7,000 integrations, the gentlest learning curve of any business platform, and reliability that IFTTT stopped matching years ago.

**What you give up:** price. The free tier of 100 tasks monthly is thin, and Starter begins at $19.99. See [getting started with Zapier](/blog/getting-started-with-zapier).

### 6. n8n — Unlimited and Self-Hosted

**Choose if:** You are technical and want no limits at a fixed cost.

Self-hosted n8n runs unlimited workflows for the price of a server, roughly $10 to $20 monthly. It also keeps data on your infrastructure, which matters if your automations touch customer records.

**What you give up:** maintenance is yours. See our [self-hosting guide](/blog/n8n-self-hosting-guide).

### 7. Pipedream — For Developers

**Choose if:** You write code and want automation that does not hide it.

JavaScript, Python, and Go inside workflow steps, with a free tier of 10,000 invocations monthly that handles real workloads.

**What you give up:** any hope that a non-technical colleague maintains it.

## When IFTTT Still Makes Sense

At $3.49 monthly, IFTTT remains the cheapest automation subscription available, and two or three well-chosen applets genuinely solve real problems. Stay if:

- **Your needs are personal and simple.** One trigger, one action, no conditions.
- **Smart home and voice are your focus** and Home Assistant sounds like more project than you want.
- **You value setup speed.** No competitor gets you running faster.

Do not keep business-critical workflows there. IFTTT has no meaningful error handling, no retry logic, and no way to tell you an applet silently stopped firing. See our [best IFTTT applets guide](/blog/best-ifttt-applets) for getting the most from the free tier before deciding.

## What is the best free alternative to IFTTT?

For smart home, Home Assistant is free, self-hosted, unlimited, and considerably more capable, though it requires setup effort. Apple Shortcuts is free and already installed if you use iPhone or Mac. For business automation, [Make](/blog/getting-started-with-make) offers 1,000 operations monthly free, which covers far more than IFTTT's two applets, and self-hosted [n8n](/blog/n8n-self-hosting-guide) is unlimited for the cost of a server. Google Home routines cover basic device control at no cost.

## Is IFTTT still worth paying for in 2026?

For personal and smart home automation, yes. At $3.49 monthly IFTTT Pro is the cheapest subscription in the category and covers voice assistants and consumer devices better than business platforms do. For business use, no. It lacks conditional logic, error handling, retries, and failure notifications, which means workflows can stop silently. Businesses should use [Make](/blog/getting-started-with-make) or [Zapier](/blog/getting-started-with-zapier) instead, as covered in our [IFTTT for business guide](/blog/ifttt-for-business).

## Can I replace IFTTT with Home Assistant?

For smart home automation, yes, and you gain considerably. Home Assistant controls devices locally so automations survive internet outages, supports far more device types, and handles conditions, loops, and time windows IFTTT cannot express. It is free and runs on a Raspberry Pi. The tradeoff is setup: expect a weekend to get comfortable and ongoing maintenance. Home Assistant does not replace IFTTT for web service automation like posting to social media, where [Make](/blog/getting-started-with-make) or Zapier fit better.
